    How these predictions are made

    Everything is calculated live in your browser. Satellite positions use the standard SGP4 model (satellite.js 6.0.2) with CelesTrak orbital elements; the Sun, Moon and planets use Astronomy Engine 2.1.19. A satellite counts as visible when it is at least 10° above the horizon, lit by the Sun, and the Sun is at least 6° below your horizon. Pass times show the visible part of each pass, as Heavens-Above does.

    Accuracy checks, with the same orbital elements: pass start, highest-point and end times agree with Skyfield using NASA JPL's DE421 ephemeris to within half a second, and directions to within 0.3°. Sun, Moon and planet rise and set times agree within 2 seconds; star positions within 1 arcminute; Voyager 1's sky position within 0.1 arcseconds of JPL Horizons. Real-world accuracy then depends mostly on the age of the orbital elements, shown at the top of the page. Brightness figures are estimates (typically ±0.5 magnitude) from standard magnitudes in Stellarium's satellite catalogue, so they can differ from Heavens-Above's; recent launches have no published brightness.

    Use your own orbital elements

    Paste two- or three-line elements (for example from celestrak.org) or load a .txt or .tle file. Each line's checksum is verified. They appear under Passes → Yours until you reload.
